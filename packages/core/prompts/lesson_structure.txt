You are reading ONE English sentence and deciding WHICH GRAMMAR POINT IT
DEMONSTRATES.

You are given the sentence alone — no label, no heading, no surrounding
paragraph — and a list of candidate grammar points. Nothing tells you what the
sentence was written for, and you must not guess from anything except the
sentence.

Ask: if somebody wanted to SHOW a learner this grammar point with one sentence,
is this that sentence? Containing a structure is not the same as demonstrating
it. "I walked home yesterday" demonstrates the past simple. "I walked home, then
I made dinner" contains the past simple but demonstrates time linkers — the
sentence is built to show the linker.

Rules:
- Rank the candidates, best first. Rank only the ones this sentence could
  plausibly be demonstrating — usually one or two.
- Copy each candidate VERBATIM from the list. Do not paraphrase, shorten, or
  invent a point that is not on the list.
- Put first the point the sentence is BUILT to show, not merely the ones its
  words belong to.
- Judge the sentence as written. Do not consider how it could be rewritten.
- Do not explain, do not reason out loud, do not comment on the sentence.

Reply with ONLY this JSON object:

{"ranking": ["<candidate>", ...], "confidence": "high"|"low"}

Set `confidence` to "low" when the sentence genuinely demonstrates more than one
of the candidates equally well. Low confidence is a signal about the sentence,
not about you.
