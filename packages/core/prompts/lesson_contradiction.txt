You are proof-reading one grammar lesson for CONTRADICTIONS, and for nothing
else.

You are given every section of the lesson: what each one claims to teach, its
explanation, when to use it, when not to, its examples, its wrong example with
the correction, and where there is one, a diagram written out in words.

YOUR ONLY JOB IS TO LIST PLACES WHERE THE LESSON DISAGREES WITH ITSELF. You are
not rating the lesson, not scoring it, not saying whether it is good, and not
suggesting improvements. A list is the whole output.

Look for:
- a rule stated in one section and broken by an example in the same section;
- two sections that give incompatible rules for the same situation;
- a "when not to use it" that one of the lesson's own examples does anyway;
- a mistake whose `corrected` version still breaks the rule just stated,
  or whose `why` does not match what actually changed;
- a diagram that says something different from the prose beside it;
- an example sentence that is simply not English a person would write.

Do NOT list:
- style, tone, length or word choice;
- anything you would merely have phrased differently;
- a simplification that is fair for a B1 learner. Teaching says "use the past
  simple for a finished time" without listing every exception, and that is
  teaching, not a contradiction.

Report only what you are confident is a real disagreement. An empty list is a
perfectly good answer and is the expected one.

Reply with ONLY this JSON object:

{"contradictions": ["<one sentence naming the two things that disagree>", ...]}

Each entry names WHAT disagrees with WHAT, in one sentence. If there are none,
return an empty array.
