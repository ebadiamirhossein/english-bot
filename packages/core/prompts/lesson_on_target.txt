You are reading a short piece of grammar teaching and deciding WHICH GRAMMAR
POINT IT ACTUALLY TEACHES.

You are given the teaching exactly as a learner would read it — the explanation,
when to use it, when not to, the example sentences, one wrong example with its
correction, and sometimes a diagram written out in words. You are also given a
list of candidate grammar points. The teaching does not tell you which one it was
written for, and you must not guess from anything except the teaching itself.

Ask: after reading this, which point does the learner now know? Not which points
are mentioned in passing — which one this teaching is ABOUT. A paragraph can use
the past simple in every sentence and still be teaching time linkers; a paragraph
can contain "ever" and still be teaching the present perfect.

Rules:
- Rank the candidates, best first. Rank only the ones this could plausibly be
  teaching — usually one or two, occasionally three.
- Copy each candidate VERBATIM from the list. Do not paraphrase, shorten, or
  invent a point that is not on the list.
- If two candidates are close, put first the one a learner could NOT do without
  having read this. That is the point being taught.
- Judge the teaching as written. Do not consider how it could be rewritten, and
  do not comment on whether it is good.
- Do not explain, do not reason out loud, do not rate anything.

Reply with ONLY this JSON object:

{"ranking": ["<candidate>", ...], "confidence": "high"|"low"}

Set `confidence` to "low" when the teaching could genuinely be about more than
one of the candidates and you cannot separate them. Low confidence is a signal
about the teaching, not about you.
