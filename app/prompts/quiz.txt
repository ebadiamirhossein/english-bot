## Spoken register — sentences people SAY

- Write sentences people SAY, not sentences people write. Test: would this
  appear in a text message or a conversation over coffee? If it sounds like a
  report or a textbook, rewrite it.
- Prefer contractions, everyday vocabulary, and short sentences.
- Use ordinary people and situations: friends, family, neighbours, colleagues
  you actually talk to. Not "the museum team", "the department", "stakeholders".
- Even work-track sentences are CONVERSATIONS about work, not documents about
  work. "Did you send Marta that pricing email yet?" — not "The pricing
  proposal was distributed to the client."
- No sentence longer than 12 words.

Bad:  "The museum team acquired an old telescope from a private collector."
Good: "My sister got me a secondhand bike for my birthday."

Bad:  "The quarterly campaign metrics were reviewed by the department."
Good: "Did you see how the Friday campaign did?"

## Formats — HARD RULE for a 5-question quiz
Exactly 2 questions must be "gap" (typed). Exactly 3 must be tapped
("choice", "spot", or "order" — mixed, never three of the same format).

With fewer than 5 questions: keep roughly 40% typed, rounding down, minimum 1
typed. Never more than two of the same format in a row.

Each due error includes a required "format" field — use EXACTLY that format
for that question. Do not swap formats.

- gap: one missing word or short phrase, typed. Use ___ for the blank.
  "accept" lists every reasonable spelling and contraction, lowercase.
- choice: exactly 4 options. All four must be plausible — no obvious throwaways.
  Distractors should be mistakes a learner at this level actually makes.
  Still include "accept" with lowercase variants of the correct option.
- spot: exactly one wrong word among the tiles. At most 8 tiles (8 words) —
  never more. "answer" is the wrong tile; "correction" is the right word.
  Prompt: "One word is wrong. Which one?"
- order: exactly 4 full-sentence options that differ ONLY in word order. One is
  natural; the others scramble the same words. Prefer for word_order errors.
  "answer" equals the correct option. Still include "accept" with lowercase
  variants of the correct sentence.

## Freshness

The same error will be quizzed again at 3, 7, 21 and 60 days. Generate a
DIFFERENT sentence each time — the learner must learn the grammar rule, not
memorise one sentence. Each due error may include "avoid_prompts": never reuse
or lightly rephrase those prompts.

Keep each prompt under 140 characters.
"answer" is the canonical form for feedback. For gap/choice/order, "accept" is
what grading matches.
