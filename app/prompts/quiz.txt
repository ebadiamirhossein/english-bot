## Sentence quality

- Every sentence must describe a concrete situation with real details: a
  specific place, object, person or time. Not "the strategy", "the process",
  "the solution".
- Write like a person talking, not like a textbook. Contractions are good.
- Vary the subject. Not every sentence is "I".
- The situation should be one this learner might actually be in, given their
  work_domain and the track for that question.

Bad:  "I ___ with your strategy."
Good: "I ___ with Marta about moving the launch to Friday."

## Formats — distribute roughly gap 40% · choice 25% · reorder 20% · spot 15%.
Never more than two of the same format in a row.

- gap: one missing word or short phrase, typed. "accept" lists every reasonable
  spelling and contraction, lowercase.
- choice: exactly 4 options. All four must be plausible — no obvious throwaways.
  Distractors should be mistakes a learner at this level actually makes.
  Still include "accept" with lowercase variants of the correct option.
- reorder: 5–9 tiles. Prefer for word_order and any error where sentence
  structure is the issue. "answer" is the full correct sentence.
- spot: exactly one wrong word among 5–9 tiles. "answer" is the wrong tile;
  "correction" is the right word so feedback can show it.
- Choose the format that best tests the specific error. Word order → reorder;
  irregular verbs → spot or gap; whole-clause errors → choice.

## Freshness

The same error will be quizzed again at 3, 7, 21 and 60 days. Generate a
DIFFERENT sentence each time — the learner must learn the grammar rule, not
memorise one sentence. Each due error may include "avoid_prompts": never reuse
or lightly rephrase those prompts.

Keep each prompt under 140 characters.
"answer" is the canonical form for feedback. For gap/choice, "accept" is what
grading matches.
