The conversation page and the video screen

Two working prototypes, phone-first, plus a board of every state and three sets of options where you asked for them. Tap through them — the thread sends, waits, records, and ends into the close-out.

What I changed and why. The two speakers now differ by three things at once: typeface (the app speaks in serif, the learner in sans), alignment, and container — the app's turns are unboxed and full-width, the learner's sit in a bordered card indented from the left, each group carrying a small text label. A machine that cannot see colour can still tell them apart. The composer opens at four lines and grows to about seven before it scrolls, because that is what people actually write. Speak and Send are now a microphone and a send glyph with the words kept as labels, so neither reads as a bare link. The entry point is a filled green button at the foot of the session, not an underlined phrase.

The close-out did not exist, so it is a page of its own, reached by ending the conversation and ordered payoff-first: what you talked about, then at most two things worth a second look, then the words you asked about with a save action on each. Corrections are amber with a triangle and the phrase worth a look — never red, never a cross. The wait state says the app is writing and nothing more. The daily limit is one sentence with no numeral in it.

On the video screen the player holds the top of the phone and shrinks to a slim bar once you read past it, so the two never compete — 1b. The tab version — 1c — gives each full height instead. The transcript is broken into timed paragraphs with a hairline per paragraph, and marked words carry a dotted underline plus a trailing square, so the mark is not colour alone. Difficulty is three words. No percentage exists anywhere in either screen.

Assumptions I made: your wedding conversation is used verbatim as the seeded thread; the video is a short kitchen-science clip standing in for a real pick; the player is a placeholder, not a live embed. Voice is hold-to-speak with an optional tap to lock, per your answer.

1lMarked words — three treatments

i · dotted underline + square

As the yeast begins to ferment the sugars, the net traps the gas.

Used in 1b. Dotted reads as tappable, the square survives greyscale, and the line of type stays quiet.

ii · tinted chip

As the yeast begins to ferment the sugars, the net traps the gas.

Unmistakably a button. Several per paragraph start to look like a form and break the reading rhythm.

iii · marginal dot, word untouched

As the yeast begins to ferment the sugars, the net traps the gas — two words here are new to you.

Cleanest text of the three, but it does not say which word, so the tap target has to be the whole line.
